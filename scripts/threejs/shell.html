The city

A working evening on a real distribution feeder. You start at the desk in the control room with four charging stations and 287 vehicles, and every lamp, meter and instrument in the city is lit by the same power-flow solution.

W A S D walks and Shift runs. ← → turn and ↑ ↓ look up and down, so the whole city is walkable with no mouse at all — or click the view to look with the mouse instead. Enter uses whatever you are facing and F acknowledges alarms. Every bus is also listed as live text below the controls.

The supervisory console on the north wall of the control room carries every setting this page has and two it does not — how many electric cars are in town, and how hard the safety layer tries. Walk to it and press Enter, or take the Go to → Supervisory desk shortcut. Keys work anywhere: 1–4 pick a controller, space or / holds and releases the day, R resets it, [ ] change speed, , . nudge the clock, G rebuilds the feeder once it is unlocked, and T hides these page controls so the room is the only interface.

It is night here at every hour of the day, on purpose. A streetlight's brightness is the voltage at its bus — that is the instrument you walk out to read — and under a daylit sky the difference between a healthy street and a browned-out one falls under the ambient and stops being visible. The sky keeps a moonlit floor so a dark street is still a street; the lamps themselves stay exactly as dark as the physics says.

Recording this? Set Picture to Full quality first. Left on Auto the page drops resolution to protect the frame rate, which is the right trade while you are exploring and the wrong one on a capture — soft type on a board you are pointing at costs more than a few dropped frames. And if you need the sandbox without walking the six stages, the escape is under Electric cars in town.

    Whether to believe any of this

    The city runs the same backward/forward sweep as the offline engine. On load it re-solves every recorded control step and compares the two, so the numbers on the meters are checked rather than trusted. Two of the four candidates are labelled stand-ins whose recorded episodes are still to be exported from the thesis notebook; they are marked as such everywhere they appear.
