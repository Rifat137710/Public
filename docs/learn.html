How a smart grid learns to charge electric cars

A town of thirty-three streets on one electricity line, and a few hundred electric cars that all want charging at the same time. Six short lessons, one live simulation, and one question at the end of it: what does a safety layer actually buy?

Measured in the thesis notebook: four charging stations, 25 evaluation episodes per method on the weak feeder, same seeds for all of them.

Violation rate here counts steps in which any street was out of range, out of 288 five-minute steps in a day. The simulator above counts street-steps out of 33 × 288, so the two columns answer slightly different questions and are not meant to be read against each other.

Bus 18 — IEEE 33-bus feeder, backward/forward sweep power flow, five-minute steps.

The power flow, the fleet accounting, the droop curve and the safety projection are the real algorithms. The two learned policies are simple stand-ins for the trained networks: what they ask for is illustrative, what the safety layer does with the request is not.
