Bus 18 · submission video · script and shot list

Six minutes thirty, and nobody will click your link

Evaluation is entirely on five slides and this video — the judges never open the live app. Everything the entry is worth has to survive inside these 390 seconds. Read the whole thing once before you record a single line.

Target 6:30 of a 5–7 min window Narration ~950 words at 150 wpm Format MP4, 1920×1080 Capture 1440×900 browser window

Why this document exists before the slides

The script is a spec for the app, not a narration of it

If a shot below turns out to be impossible, that is a build task discovered today rather than a disaster discovered on Friday with your microphone already set up. Walk the six stages with this open and check every on screen block against what actually happens. Anything that does not match, tell me — there is still time to change the app.

Before you record

  • Browser at 1440 × 900, full screen, no bookmarks bar, no extensions visible.
  • Reload so you start at stage 1 with an empty map.
  • Capture screen and voice separately. Screen first, silent, as many takes as you need; voice afterwards against the cut. Trying to do both at once is how six-minute videos take nine hours.
  • The day speed buttons are your edit tool — ×64 for the parts nobody needs to watch in real time, ×4 for the moments that matter.
  • Every number below is what the build currently produces. If a take shows something different, the number changed and this script is wrong — tell me rather than reading the old figure.

The cut

0:0025 s

Cold open — the lights go out

On screen Village only, no cursor. Evening, around 19:00, safety layer off. All four stations pulled hard into charge. The far end of the feeder dims and the lamps at bus 18 begin to flicker. Hold on the dark end for a beat. Cut to title card: Bus 18.

"This is a village at the end of a long electricity line. Two hundred and eighty-seven electric cars just came home and plugged in. Nobody did anything wrong. The lights are going out anyway."

Direction

Say nothing for the first four seconds. Let it get dark first.

0:2535 s

The problem, and the theme

On screen Pull back to the whole screen. Cursor traces the feeder from the substation outward, then rests on the voltage profile showing the sag below the red line.

"Rural feeders were built for lights and fans, not for cars. The further you are from the substation, the worse it gets — and the people at the far end have no idea their voltage is anyone's decision. Bus 18 is a browser trainer that puts you in charge of that decision, on the IEEE thirty-three bus benchmark feeder, with real power flow running underneath. Sustainable Smart Cities and Urban Innovation."

Required

The theme category must be said aloud and appear on screen. It is a submission requirement.

1:0065 s

Stage 1 — you drive, and you fail

On screen Stage 1 brief card. Then: grab a charging station in the village itself and drag it down. Show the number moving in the street card and the console slider following. Run the day at ×16. Evening arrives, the far end collapses. Land on the reveal card.

"You get four charging stations and no help. Drag one where it stands — this is the actual feeder, so the moment you pull power the voltage everywhere else moves. Charge too hard and the far end goes dark. Charge too little and people go home with empty batteries, and every one of those cars had enough time plugged in to be full. So that's a decision you made, not a shortage. Almost nobody threads it on the first run. That failure is the point — you cannot be impressed by a solution to a problem you have not personally felt."

Direction

Use the village drag, not the sliders. It is the strongest hands-on moment in the entry and it is the thing a judge has not seen before.

2:0540 s

Stages 2 and 3 — the two corners

On screen Uncoordinated at ×64, dot lands on the map. Then droop at ×64, second dot. Cut to the map with both dots and the learner's own.

"Here is what happens today with no coordination: everyone charges at full power on plug-in. Service nought point nine eight three — nearly everybody charged — and thirty-two percent of the day spent illegal. Now the standard answer, the volt-watt droop curve from IEEE 1547, in every inverter sold. Violations fall to nought point nought four five. Service collapses to nought point five six. A rule that only knows its own bus protects the grid by refusing to use it."

2:4550 s

Stage 4 — the credibility beat

On screen SAC-Lag runs. Its dot lands below and to the right of droop. Hold on the map. Zoom the two dots if your editor allows.

"So put a neural network on it. Deep reinforcement learning, full system state, millions of steps of training. It lands here — worse on safety and worse on service than a rule written down in a standards document. There is no axis on which it wins. This is the finding the underlying research reported, and it is the reason this trainer exists. Intelligence was not the missing ingredient. The agent could see everything about the system except how its own actions move voltage through the network."

Direction

Slow down here. This is the beat that earns the entry its credibility — it is a result that surprises experts.

3:3545 s

Stage 5 — the physics goes back in

On screen SafeSAC runs. Its dot lands up and to the left of everything. Then toggle Safety projection OFF and re-run so the dot moves — showing how hard the layer had been working.

"Same backbone, same training budget, one addition. At every control step a safety layer measures how much each station actually moves the voltage at every bus on the live network, and projects the request onto the nearest command the feeder can survive. Nought point nought one six violations, nought point five five service — it beats the plain agent by nearly thirty points of service at lower risk. And that layer was running while it trained, so this is a different learned policy, not the old one behind a filter."

4:2055 s

Stage 6 — the trap

On screen The procurement table: violation rate and net cost only. Cursor hovers the winning row — best safety, only profit — and clicks Deploy this. The third column appears: 0.000. Hold on that zero.

"Now you're the engineer on the procurement call. Two columns, the two a manager always asks for: how often does it break the rules, and what does it cost. One controller has the best safety record on the board and is the only one that makes money. Most people deploy it. Here is the column nobody asked for. It served nobody. Not one vehicle, all day. It earned a perfect safety score by never operating — and in the field you would never find out, because reality does not hand you the counterfactual. Whenever you are shown a safety number, ask what it cost in service."

Direction

Two full seconds of silence on the 0.000 before the next line. This is the entry's thesis.

5:1530 s

Debrief and the energy story

On screen Debrief screen. Scroll past the map and findings to the energy finding, then the eight objectives, then click Save one-page summary and show the exported document opening.

"The debrief puts every run in one frame and names the eight objectives explicitly. Including one that comes free from the physics: losses rise with the square of current, so coordinating the fleet burned six kilowatt-hours in the wires per driver served, against twelve for uncoordinated charging. Efficiency is not a fourth objective competing with the other two. It falls out of getting the first two right."

5:4530 s

Evidence

On screen A single clean results card. Numbers only — no app footage competing with them.

"We ran this with n engineers and engineering students. Concept scores went from pre to post out of ten. System Usability Scale SUS, against an average of sixty-eight. And x of n deployed the controller that served nobody — which is the point. The misconception is not hypothetical. It is sitting in the population being taught."

Blocked on the tester sessions

Every blank is filled from the tally sheet in the facilitator pack. Record this shot last. If the pick rate comes out low, we say the real number and change the sentence — a study that can only come out one way is not a study.

6:1515 s

Close

On screen The village at dusk with the lights holding steady. URL on screen, large, held long enough to read twice.

"No headset, no install, no account. It runs in a browser on a phone, which matters when the people who most need to understand weak feeders are standing next to one. Bus 18."

Do not do this

Never narrate a number the screen is not showing

A judge watching a 6:30 video with a scoring sheet will notice a mismatch, and one mismatch costs more than any shot gains. If the take shows nought point nought five seven and the script says nought point nought five, reshoot or change the line. Do not round toward the story.
